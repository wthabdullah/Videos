Step 2 · Sort

Step 2 of 3

Sort and use.

Sorting keeps waste worth something. Mixed, it is worth nothing.

Three colours: green, blue, red.No coloured bins? Three bags or boxes will do. Use the same colours at home and on your street.
GREEN

Organic

Peels, food scraps, leaves, garden waste.
Make compostMix with dry leaves or torn paper. Keep it damp, turn it now and then, and wait.
BLUE

Recyclable

Paper, cardboard, cans, glass, plastic bottles and tubs.
Sell itKeep it clean and dry. Sell it to a scrap dealer or take it to a collection point.
RED

Special

Batteries, electronics, chemicals, medicines, sharp things, sanitary waste.
Keep it apartIn its own bag, wrapped. Ask your local authority where to hand it in.

Respect the people who handle our waste

Wrap broken glass and needles. Sort properly. Treat waste workers with dignity.

5WASTE TO WEALTH
From one home to one street

The 8-week action plan.

A home that sorts has a habit. A street that sorts can ask for sorted collection.

1

Identify

Weeks 1–2
  • Make a team of young people and community members. Give everyone a responsibility.
  • Find waste collection points, and the recyclable materials common on your street.
2

Show

Weeks 3–4
  • With permission, install bins in the three colours at the collection points.
  • Hold a demonstration: sort a heap of mixed waste in public. Explain everyone’s moral and legal duty to sort.
3

Coordinate

Weeks 5–6
  • Contact the local authority and waste collectors. Decide who keeps each area clean.
  • Ask shopkeepers, schools and housing societies for support.
  • Meet scrap dealers and recyclers. Ask about prices and collection.
4

Advocate

Weeks 7–8
  • Campaign for sorted collection, so sorted waste stays sorted in the truck.
  • Ask the municipality, or its contractor, to own waste collection on your street.
Your result: do the 7-day challenge in week 1 and week 8. The difference is what your street changed.

The law
The law

What the law says.

Each province and region has its own law to protect the environment and control pollution and waste.

Islamabad
Pakistan Environmental Protection Act 1997; Single-Use Plastics Regulations 2023
CoversPollution, hazardous waste, polythene bags and other single-use plastic.
Up to Rs 1 million
Repeat offence: up to 2 years in prison
Punjab
Punjab Environmental Protection Act 1997, amended 2012
CoversPollution, hazardous waste.
Up to Rs 5 million
Repeat offence: up to 2 years in prison
Sindh
Sindh Environmental Protection Act 2014
CoversPollution, dumping waste in unauthorised places, non-degradable plastic.
Up to Rs 5 million
Repeat offence: up to 3 years in prison
Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
KP Environmental Protection Act 2014; Amendment Act 2022
CoversPollution, hazardous waste, plastic bags.
Rs 50,000 to Rs 5 million
Repeat offence: up to 2 years in prison
Balochistan
Balochistan Environmental Protection Act 2012
CoversPollution; hazardous, electronic, solid and hospital waste.
Up to Rs 1 million
Repeat offence: up to 2 years in prison
Gilgit-Baltistan
Gilgit-Baltistan Environmental Protection Act 2014
CoversPollution, hazardous waste.
Up to Rs 1 million
Repeat offence: up to 2 years in prison
Azad Jammu and Kashmir
AJK Environmental Protection Act 2000
CoversPollution, hazardous waste, used polythene bags.
Up to Rs 1 million
Repeat offence: up to 2 years in prison
At home, the rule is simple: don’t burn waste, don’t dump it in drains or empty plots, and keep special waste apart.
